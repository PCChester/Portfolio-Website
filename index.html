Good work gets lost in the explanation.

Most specialists were trained to get things right, not to get them across. The cost shows up in meetings, inboxes and steering committees.

  • The analysis is right.The steering committee still asks to see it again next month.
  • The proposal is thorough.The client signs with someone who made it simpler.
  • The engineer knows the fix.The meeting ends without a decision.

None of that is a knowledge problem. It's a communication problem, and it can be trained.

How a course comes together

  1. A short conversation

    You tell me about the team, the work, and where communication is costing you time. No forms, no sales process.

  2. A course incorporating your material

    I adapt the course to your people's real presentations, reports and meetings, and agree the format with you.

  3. Practice, feedback and a plan

    Participants practise on their own work, get specific feedback, and leave with a plan for the next 90 days.

Tell me about your team.

A short email is enough: who they are, what they present or write, and where it isn't working. I'll reply with an honest view and a suggested course.
