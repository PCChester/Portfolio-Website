Communication

Two kinds of communication, one family. Corporate communication covers the messages an organisation sends as a whole: strategy, reputation, crisis and stakeholder relations. Business communication covers the work people do every day: presenting, persuading, writing to clients and holding the difficult conversation. Every course incorporates participants' own materials for real-world practice.

Presenting for Decisions

Plan, build and deliver a presentation that ends with a decision.

Who it's for

Managers, project leads and client-facing staff, and the technical, finance and data specialists who present to people outside their field.

Participants leave able to
  • Say what they want the audience to decide before they open a slide deck
  • Build the case in three moves (what they're saying, what supports it, what follows) and cut the rest
  • Show numbers so a non-specialist reaches the right conclusion
  • Control voice, pace and nerves, in the room and on camera
  • Answer hard questions, and cut twenty minutes to five when asked
They take away

A finished, rehearsed presentation of their own, recorded practice with scored feedback, and a method for building the next one.

AI adoption

For organisations that have the tools but not yet the habits. These courses cover briefing AI well and checking what it produces, finding the work worth handing over, and building the internal champions who spread good practice. They are tool-nonspecific and built around participants' real tasks.

Briefing AI Well: Judgment, Delegation and Quality Control

Hand work to AI the way you'd hand it to a capable new colleague: clearly, selectively, and with your name still on the result.

Who it's for

Managers, specialists and professionals in any function who already use AI tools and want consistently better results from them, with less rework and less risk.

Participants leave able to
  • Decide which tasks to hand to AI, which to share with it, and which to keep
  • Write a brief that gives AI the context, constraints and standard a good colleague would need
  • Check AI output for errors, invented facts and confident nonsense before anyone else sees it
  • Protect confidential and personal data in everyday AI use
  • Say honestly how AI contributed to their work, to managers, clients and colleagues
They take away

A personal library of tested briefs for the tasks they do most, a checklist for reviewing AI output, and their own working setup, built and tried on their real work during the course.

Data storytelling

For the people who do the analysis and the people who rely on it: turning numbers into recommendations, reports and dashboards that decision-makers understand and act on.

Presenting Analysis to Decision-Makers

Turn findings into a recommendation that a non-specialist understands, trusts and acts on.

Who it's for

Data analysts, BI and finance specialists, engineers and researchers who present results to managers, clients or boards outside their field.

Participants leave able to
  • Start from the decision the audience has to make, not from the data they have
  • Turn an analysis into a story: the situation, what changed, what it means, what to do
  • Choose the chart that makes the point, and strip out everything that hides it
  • Explain uncertainty, assumptions and limitations without losing the room's confidence
  • Handle challenge from people who don't like the answer

A presentation built from their own analysis, rehearsed with feedback, and a one-page method for turning the next analysis into a recommendation.

Reports and Dashboards That Get Used

Written analysis and dashboards that answer the reader's question in the first ten seconds.
